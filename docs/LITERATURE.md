# Related work and novelty risks

1. Jiang et al. (2025), **HypoChainer: A Collaborative System Combining LLMs and Knowledge Graphs for Hypothesis-Driven Scientific Discovery**, IEEE TVCG. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2025.3633887. Already includes validation selection: **do not** claim this concept as new.
2. Husain et al. (2021), **A Multi-Scale Visual Analytics Approach for Exploring Biomedical Knowledge**, VAHC. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/VAHC53616.2021.00010. Multiscale means graph-level and ontology zoom, not explicitly molecule-to-organ verification prioritization.
3. He et al. (2026), **MedViz**, preprint. DOI: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2601.20709. Agent-based visual biomedical literature exploration and hypothesis generation.
4. Corvó et al. (2020), **Visual Analytics for Hypothesis-Driven Exploration in Computational Pathology**, IEEE TVCG. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2020.2990336.
5. von Wyl and Bernard (2025), **Grand Challenge: Mediating Between Confirmatory and Exploratory Research Cultures in Health Sciences and Visual Analytics**. DOI: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.11544.

**Candidate gap (not proof of novelty):** explicitly prioritizing *verification tasks across biological levels* under a cost budget, with traceable reasons and an evaluation against source-backed independent critical-gap labels.

Investigate whether newer systems combine biological scales, evidence provenance, information gain and verification cost; conduct more exhaustive literature search before novelty claims. HRA anatomical links must not be interpreted as disease causality.
